## Interpretation

1. **Keep-alive is the dominant mechanism.** Going greedy → greedy+keep-alive cuts
   cold starts 79% (100u) / 18% (500u) and turnaround ~53% / 11%. warm_rate jumps
   0.20→0.83 (100u). This is the headline ablation result and it is statistically
   solid (CIs do not overlap with greedy).

2. **Prediction placement and prewarm add only a small increment.** keep-alive →
   prediction(no-warm) → predictive improves cold starts further by ~1 percentage
   point at a time. At n=5 these three warm variants are **within each other's CIs**
   — i.e., the differences AMONG them are *not* statistically significant here.
   Do not claim "predictive ≫ keep-alive"; the honest claim is "predictive is best
   but its margin over keep-alive is small / within noise at this scale."

3. **Benefit shrinks as load grows** (100u → 500u): as users saturate the 10 edges
   and the 32-function pool, reuse opportunity falls, so every variant's advantage
   over greedy compresses (cold-start reduction 79% → 18%). The 1000u/5000u columns
   will show whether this trend continues.

## Caveats
- **Tail latency (p95/p99) CIs are large for the warm variants at 100u** (±300–370 ms):
  the tail flips between a warm (~420 ms) and cold (~1470 ms) request depending on
  seed (bimodal), so the mean tail is unstable at n=5. Greedy's tail is tight
  because it is almost always cold.
- **predictive's p95/p99 are slightly *worse* at 500u** (1498/1567 vs ~1473/1487):
  the prewarm switch adds migration/handoff cost to some requests' tail. Worth a
  sentence in §V if it persists at scale.
- **Differences among keep-alive / pred-noWS / predictive are within CI** (see point 2).
